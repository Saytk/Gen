## 0. What the camera actually shows (read this first)

Every rule below follows from these figures. They are all **[GEN calc]**, derived from the project camera at 16:9. Check them in PIE.

| Quantity | Formula | Value | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground width at the focus point | 2·1700·tan(25°) | ≈ 1585 uu (~16 m) | Small arenas. Every square metre of floor is on screen a lot |
| Vertical FOV | 2·atan(tan 25° · 9/16) | ≈ 29.4° | — |
| Ground depth through the screen centre | camera height 1700·sin 60° ≈ 1472 uu; near and far rays at −74.7° / −45.3° | ≈ 4.5 m nearer than the focus, ≈ 6.1 m beyond (≈ 10.5 m total) | The arena rim only enters the frame when the player stands within ~6–8 m of an edge |
| Top-of-frame ray | −(60° − 14.7°) | ≈ −45.3° | **The horizon never shows.** The sky matters only as the source of sky-light colour and ambient |
| On-screen density at the focus | 1080 px / (2·1700·tan 14.7°) | ≈ 1.2 px/cm (1080p) · 1.6 (1440p) · 2.4 (4K) | Anything under ~4 px (~3–5 cm) disappears |
| Floor area to screen | 121 px/m × (121·sin 60°) px/m | **1 m² ≈ 0.6 % of a 1080p screen** | Used for the VFX area caps in §7.2 |
| Height of a 180 cm hero on screen | 180 cm × cos 60° × 1.21 px/cm | ≈ 110 px at 1080p | Faces, fingers and belts don't exist. Head, shoulders and weapon are the whole character |
| Head size at 5.5 heads tall | 110 / 5.5 | ≈ 20 px at 1080p | One shape and one colour per head, no more |

**Rule 0 [GEN]:** judge every asset **in a gameplay capture from this camera, with 6 heroes and spells on screen, and in motion**. Never judge from a viewport beauty shot. There is precedent for the "in motion" part: Stunlock's previous game, Bloodline Champions, was described as plain in screenshots but good in motion ([Ten Ton Hammer](https://www.tentonhammer.com/articles/bloodline-champions-pc-review)).

## 1. Vision statement and pillars

**Vision.** Gen looks like a bright, soft-cartoon arena sport. Chunky heroes with painterly colours stand on a calm, mid-value floor. Every spell's shape, size and colour tell you exactly what it does and whose it is. As at Battlerite, the art's first job is to "support and visually explain the gameplay" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/battlerite-interiew)).

Gen adds one signature on top: **a cool-shadow toon ramp with a warm terminator band** (the 12 fps VFX stepping first proposed here no longer applies to spell VFX: they are smooth, **[TASTE #5]**). One review said Battlerite's style "very closely matches that of other MOBAs" ([KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/battlerite-review/)), and this signature is how Gen avoids that.

| # | Pillar | One-line test |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Gameplay first.** Visuals equal hitboxes; loudness equals threat | Would a new player know what to dodge from a single frame? |
| P2 | **Readable at 110 px.** Big shapes, top-down silhouettes, no micro-detail | Does it pass the black-silhouette and greyscale tests from the game camera? |
| P3 | **Soft toon, not anime ink.** 2–3 band ramp, cool shadows, painterly flat albedo | Do shadows ever go black or grey? (They must not.) |
| P4 | **Quiet stage, loud actors.** Environment < characters < VFX < UI | Is the floor the least interesting thing in a teamfight capture? |
| P5 | **Two-person scope.** One master material per family; palette swaps instead of repaints | Can a new arena or skin be made mostly by editing curves and parameters? |
| P6 | **Performance is a feature.** Every visual rule fits the frame budget (§3.0); when they clash, change the visual's implementation, not the budget | Does the clutter scene (§10 #4) hold the target fps on the min-spec tier? |

**Visual hierarchy, top wins** ([Riot, Summoner's Rift update](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2014/06/update-to-summoners-rift-headed-to-pbe.html), [Spellbreak](https://80.lv/articles/spellbreak-developing-a-magic-battle-royale-rpg)):
**UI > ability VFX and telegraphs > characters > interactables (orbs) > floor > arena walls and backdrop.**

How this works in practice [GEN]:
- **Outside impact frames**, heroes are the highest-contrast elements that are not VFX.
- **During impact frames (2–4 frames)**, only the VFX primary layers may beat hero contrast.
- The floor and walls never beat the heroes.

## 2. What we take from Battlerite, and what we do differently

Sources on Battlerite's look:
- It uses "a softer, more cartoony art style than its predecessor" ([mmos.com](https://mmos.com/news/stunlock-studios-gives-us-our-first-look-at-battlerite)). Steam players had described Bloodline Champions as dark and unappealing ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/504370/discussions/0/343787920115631200)).
- It was built in Unity 5. Stunlock credits Unity's extensible rendering pipeline for "our own touch in terms of art style" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/battlerite-interiew), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlerite)).
- Its minimum spec was DX9.0c on Intel HD 3000 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/504370/Battlerite/)).
- The pipeline was a ZBrush sculpt, then a low-poly game mesh, then textures hand-painted in 3D-Coat ([Stunlock Dev Blog 001](https://blog.stunlock.com/dev-blog-001/)).

## 3. Rendering and shading spec (UE 5.8.3)

### 3.0 Performance budget (overrides every visual rule in this document) [GEN]

A competitive game lives on frame time and input latency. The cartoon look suits this, because it needs no Lumen, no ray tracing, no high-poly detail and no film post-processing. These rules make sure nothing in the bible works against it:

- **Cheapest passing option wins.** Every A/B test here (GI, shadows, outlines, anti-aliasing) keeps the **cheapest** configuration that passes the readability tests. Visual preference alone never justifies a more expensive option, unless it still fits the budget.
- **Conflicts.** If a visual rule cannot fit the budget, simplify its *implementation*: a cheaper shader, fewer particles, a baked texture instead of a runtime effect. Keep the readability result, and log the change in §13.
- **Measure, don't assume.** Profile in a **packaged Development or Test build**, not PIE (PIE carries editor overhead). Use the §10 #4 clutter scene at 1080p. Tools: `stat unit`, `stat gpu`, `ProfileGPU` and Unreal Insights.
- **Profile from day one.** Each new master material, Niagara system or post-process pass gets a before/after `stat gpu` capture in its PR.

**Starting GPU budget at the default target** (120 fps = 8.3 ms on a mid-range GPU at 1080p, High tier; §12 Q2). These are split guides, not law:

| Pass group | Budget | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base pass + depth prepass | ≤ 1.5 ms | ~6 heroes + a small arena. Keep material count and draw calls low (§6.2) |
| Shadows | ≤ 1.0 ms | One shadow-casting directional light. VSM vs CSM decided by measurement (§3.6) |
| Lighting + sky + AO | ≤ 1.0 ms | GI None + DFAO in config A |
| Translucency + VFX | ≤ 1.5 ms | Worst case is the clutter scene; overdraw limits in §7.8 |
| Post (outline, bloom, tonemap) + TSR | ≤ 1.5 ms | Outline ≤ 0.5 ms; TSR is the biggest item here |
| UI | ≤ 0.3 ms | — |
| **Headroom** | **≥ 1.5 ms** | Absorbs spikes; never planned away |

- **CPU and game thread:** ≤ 4 ms at 120 fps for 6 heroes. Watch per-frame `Tick` work, Niagara spawn bursts and GameplayCue spawning (pool everything, §7.8).
  - **Animation [GEN, unvalidated]:** client anim update + evaluation for 6 heroes ≤ ~1 ms of game-thread time (§8.5).
  - **Dedicated server:** take its numbers from a `-server` process (or, with a source build, a packaged server) with Unreal Insights, **never from PIE**. PIE runs animation multithreaded and skips `IsRunningDedicatedServer()` guards (§8.5). The server budget is still undefined (§12 Q30).
- **Min-spec (Low tier):** 60 fps (16.6 ms) on a low-end GPU with the §3.8 Low settings.
- **Memory:** hero textures 1024² by default (§5.5); mesh distance fields only on environment meshes that actually need DFAO.

### 3.2 Material architecture

Pin the profile **on the Toon BSDF node in each master**, not through Material Instance overrides. In 5.8, overrides are lost after an editor restart ([forum bug](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ue-5-8-substrate-toon-profile-override-in-material-instance-fails-to-apply-after-restarting-the-editor/2738757)). That means one master per profile.

| Master | Toon Profile | Used for | Key inputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `M_Toon_Char` | `TP_Character` | Heroes, weapons | BaseColor (flat albedo + vertical gradient); Emissive (rim + team/hit flash); Roughness ~0.8 constant; Metallic 0 except accents |
| `M_Toon_Env` | `TP_Environment` | Floor, walls, rim kit | Curve Atlas gradient, world-aligned blend, vertex colour or RVT tint |
| `M_Toon_Prop` | `TP_Prop` | Orbs, obstacles, props | Like Env, with slightly higher contrast |
| `M_VFX_Erode` | — (Unlit) | Round-flow and environment erosion only (§7.9); **not spell VFX [TASTE #5]**, which use the Stylized `M_ST_*` / `NS_ST_*` family | See §7.4 |
| `M_VFX_Telegraph` | — (Unlit) | Ground indicators, team rings | See §7.5 |

Keep static switches to a minimum, because each one multiplies client shader permutations. Rendering choices cost nothing on the dedicated server, which has no RHI; materials only affect client cook size and shader compile time ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/packaging-and-creating-a-dedicated-server/280235)).

### 3.3 Toon Profile starting values [GEN]

How the engine evaluates the profile [FACT, local source]:
- The diffuse ramp samples **half-Lambert** (`NoL*0.5+0.5`) (`Engine/Shaders/Private/Substrate/SubstrateToonBSDF.ush:140`).
- With "Diffuse Ramp Includes Shadow" on, shadowing is multiplied in before the ramp lookup, so cast shadows snap to the same bands.
- Specular is a GGX D-only term passed through the specular ramp.
- Properties are listed in `Engine/Classes/Engine/ToonProfile.h`.

This is the same idea as Valve's TF2 warp ramp ([NPAR 2007](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall07/cos597B/papers/mitchell-team-fortress.pdf)).

| Setting | `TP_Character` | `TP_Environment` | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diffuse ramp bands | 3: shadow / warm terminator / lit | 2: shadow / lit | **[GEN]** choice. One tutorial suggests 3 bands for a mid-tone ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/substrate-toon-shader-ue5-8-tutorial)) |
| Band edge softness | 0.10–0.15 (half-Lambert units) | 0.15–0.20 | A soft, "Battlerite" edge, not anime-hard |
| Shadow colour (multiplier) | ≈ (0.62, 0.66, 0.85), cool lavender | ≈ (0.60, 0.65, 0.80) | **Never black or grey** ([TF2](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall07/cos597B/papers/mitchell-team-fortress.pdf)) |
| Terminator band | ~0.05 wide, tint ≈ (1.0, 0.82, 0.72), slightly more saturated | None | Our signature warm edge (after TF2's reddened terminator); skin gets the reddest tint |
| Lit colour | 1.0 (albedo as authored) | 1.0 | — |
| Diffuse Ramp Includes Shadow | **On** | **On** | Cast shadows snap to the same bands |
| Specular ramp | One small, hard blob, on metal and hair only | Off | — |
| Dither / hatching | Off | Off (optional AO-only hatching later, [Hi-Fi Rush idea](https://80.lv/articles/the-making-of-hi-fi-rush-s-3d-toon-rendering-style)) | Needs PatternUVs wired |
| Diffuse Indirect Scale | 0.3–0.6 (only if Lumen is kept) | 0.4–0.6 | Stops bounce light washing out the bands (applied in local `DiffuseIndirectComposite.usf`) |
| Specular Indirect Scale | 0–0.2 | 0–0.2 | Rough toon surfaces gain little |

Limits [FACT, local `ToonProfile.h` / `ToonProfile.cpp`]:
- At most 256 profiles, because the profile ID is 8 bits.
- Atlas resolution is set by `r.Substrate.ToonProfile.Resolution` (default 64).
- Gen uses 2–3 profiles in total.

**Rim ("sun-cap") [GEN, after TF2].** In the `M_Toon_Char` Emissive, add `Fresnel(1−N·V)^4 × saturate(N·Up)` × a rim colour (warm sky tint), at an intensity of ~0.1–0.25. From a −60° camera, heads and shoulders face upward, so a rim weighted towards the up direction separates heroes from the floor better than a view-only rim ([TF2 paper](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall07/cos597B/papers/mitchell-team-fortress.pdf)).

### 3.4 Outlines

**v0: Custom Stencil post-process outline, characters only, team-coloured.**

- **Setup.** Project Settings → Custom Depth-Stencil Pass = **Enabled with Stencil**. Stencil values: **1 = self, 2 = ally, 3 = enemy**, set in C++ on each client, because team is relative to the viewer ([Tom Looman](https://tomlooman.com/unreal-engine-outline-multi-color-post-process/)).
- **Blendable location: Scene Color Before DOF.** This runs at render resolution **before TSR** (local `PostProcessing.cpp:962`; `BlendableInterface.h`), so lines get jittered and then anti-aliased by TSR. Check for flicker with `r.TSR.Visualize` ([TSR docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/temporal-super-resolution-in-unreal-engine), [post-process docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/post-process-materials-in-unreal-engine)).
- **Draw order with VFX.** The pass also runs **after translucency**, so outlines draw on top of spell VFX that pass in front of heroes. **v0 decision [GEN]:** keep this behaviour, because it keeps hero positions readable in the clutter test (§10 #4). Revisit if it looks wrong on impact frames (§12).
- **Width.** **2 px at 1080p**, minimum 1.5 px at render resolution; thinner lines flicker under TSR. Smoothstep the edge term instead of a hard step.
- **Occluded fill.** Optional, at ~25 % alpha, for heroes behind pillars.
- **Custom Depth only on heroes** (and hero-attached weapons). Never on environment, props or VFX, so the extra depth pass stays at ~6 skinned meshes.
- **One post-process material** does the outline, the occluded fill and the team colours together. Use the cheapest edge kernel that looks clean (4-tap cross before 8-tap Sobel).
- **Budget.** Profile with `stat gpu`; target < 0.5 ms at 1080p [GEN].

**Phase 2 (only if the look needs ink): inverted hull through `OverlayMaterial`** ([ASW slides](https://docswell.com/s/ASW_Academy/5LVY67-GG-Toonline-Eng), [Epic Japan](https://docswell.com/s/EpicGamesJapan/5DEVPV-2023-12-01-082936)):
- Extrude in WPO along **smoothed normals baked into vertex colour RGB or a spare UV**. Store thickness in vertex alpha (0 = no line, 0.5 = standard, 1 = 2×).
- Line colour = the albedo, darkened and saturated. **Never pure black.**
- On static meshes the overlay draws with `CastShadow=false` (local `StaticMeshSceneProxy.cpp`). **Check the same behaviour in the skeletal-mesh proxy** before relying on it for heroes.
- Risk: translucent overlays don't write velocity, so they may smear under TSR. Test against an opaque two-sided hull.
- Keep heroes **non-Nanite**, because of the overlay/inverted-hull workflow and skinning.

### 3.6 Lumen A/B test protocol

Run in `L_Arena` at 1080p from the gameplay camera, with 6 heroes and 6 ultimates firing.

| Config | Setup |
|---|---|
| **A** (preferred) | `DynamicGlobalIlluminationMethod=None`, `r.ReflectionMethod=0`, Movable directional light, Movable sky light + DFAO (needs Mesh Distance Fields) |
| **B** | `DynamicGlobalIlluminationMethod=Lumen` + **`r.Lumen.FinalGatherMethod=0`** (Irradiance Field Gather, "Lumen Lite", described in source as targeting mid-range PC). `r.ReflectionMethod=0`. Epic claims it is ~2× faster than Lumen High ([5.8 notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes), relayed by [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/lumen-vs-lumen-lite-ue5-8-performance)). **Measure it; don't assume it** |
| **C** | Full Lumen GI (default final gather), `r.ReflectionMethod=0`, Toon Diffuse Indirect Scale 0.3–0.6, Local Exposure on |
| **Shadow sub-test** | Run the winning config twice: **VSM (SMRT 0)** vs **CSM** (2–3 cascades, dynamic shadow distance ≈ 3000 uu, which covers the ~16 m view). Keep the cheaper one if both pass |
| **D** (optional, only if A misses the budget) | Baked lighting (GPU Lightmass) + one Stationary directional light for hero shadows. Cheapest at runtime, but it needs `r.AllowStaticLighting=True` (currently `False`; turning it on adds shader permutations project-wide) and a re-bake per lighting variant |

Lumen Lite is not a separate GI method. In 5.8.3, `EDynamicGlobalIlluminationMethod` only offers None, Lumen, ScreenSpace (deprecated) and Plugin.

| Metric | Pass |
|---|---|
| GPU frame time | Fits the §3.0 pass budgets in a packaged build. For reference, Epic's [Lumen budget is 4 ms at High for 60 fps](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine), which alone is half of our 120 fps frame |
| Band stability | No shimmer in the dark bands while the camera moves ([community report](https://unrealcommunity.wiki/cel-shading-post-process-6t8vztk6)) |
| Greyscale test (§10 #1) | Heroes still separate from the floor |
| Spell lights | Fireball light reads on the floor without blowing out the bands |

Keep the cheapest config that passes all four.

### 3.7 Step-by-step prototype plan

Per `CLAUDE.md`, run `git pull` and `git lfs lock` on every `.uasset` or `.umap` before touching it (§3.9).

1. **Align engine builds.** Both team members register the same 5.8.3 build under one shared GUID, or use the launcher's "5.8" association. Commit the `.uproject` association once (§12 Q1). **Never commit an unrelated `EngineAssociation` change.**

4. **Cvars.** SMRT = 0, `r.ReflectionMethod=0`. Capture a baseline.
5. **Stencil outline** with self, ally and enemy colours. Check for jitter while moving.
6. **Lumen A/B test** (§3.6). Record frame times and captures in the PR.
7. **`M_Toon_Env` floor** with a Curve Atlas, plus one wall-kit piece.
8. **`M_VFX_Telegraph`** (unlit). Check the border value in the sun band and the shadow band (§10 #13).
9. **Fireball at the GAS collision radius**, with a debug sphere. ~~Rebuild on `M_VFX_Erode`~~: superseded by **[TASTE #5]**; the Stylized `NS_ST_Fireball` family is the reference look (§7.4).
10. **Visual-target hero.** Take one hero to final quality (ramp, outline, palette, VFX, animations). Every later hero is measured against it ([Overwatch's Torbjörn approach](https://www.cookandbecker.com/en/article/378/designing-overwatch.html)).
11. **PIE with 2 clients + a dedicated server.** Verify per-viewer team colours, outlines and cosmetic hit-stop with no movement corrections. Run the full §10 checklist, then the network emulation pass from §8.4 #9 (Average profile, then Bad + `NetEmulation.PktLoss 10`, with `p.NetShowCorrections 1` and `net.Montage.Debug 1`).
12. Freeze v1 of this bible with measured numbers.

### 3.8 Scalability tiers [GEN]

Battlerite ran on DX9 / Intel HD 3000 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/504370/Battlerite/)). Gen won't go that low, but the look must hold on Low.

## 4. Colour and value

### 4.1 Palette strategy

| Layer | Hue family | Albedo value (HSV V, sRGB) | Albedo saturation | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arena floor | Cool or neutral (blue-grey, slate, desaturated moss), or warm-neutral on warm-themed arenas | **25–55 % overall; any single arena's floor stays within a band ≤ 20 points wide** | **≤ 35 %** | Narrowest value range on screen ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)) |
| Walls, rim, backdrop | Same family as the floor, darker | 15–50 % | ≤ 40 % | Detail is allowed; contrast is not |
| Characters | Any hue family **except the reserved team hues as a dominant colour** (§4.3) | 35–85 % | **40–75 %** | Hero upper body must contrast with the floor: mean V ≥ the floor band's maximum + 15 points, **or** opposite hue temperature (cool hero on a warm floor, warm hero on a cool floor) with ≥ 10 points of V. **[GEN]** (inspired by, not stated in, [Dev Blog 32](https://blog.stunlock.com/dev-blog-32/)) |
| Interactables (orbs) | Energy amber for both Gen orbs: the centre orb (energy + a small heal) and the death orb (energy only), per CharacterGuidelines §4.2. Green stays for heal effects only. (Battlerite used green for health orbs, [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/battlerite-is-a-true-moba-and-thank-god-1820989224).) Hex values in §4.3 | 60–90 % | 60–85 % | — |
| VFX primary core | Element colour | **In flight: V 75–85 %, S ≤ 85 %. Impact frames (2–4) only: may exceed 85 %, clip to white or bloom** | Same split | ([VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)); tier limits in §7.2 |
| Team channel | See §4.3 | — | — | Reserved hues |

All ranges are **[GEN] proposals**. Calibrate them against measured Battlerite and LoL captures (§12 Q14).

**Hard rules**
- **Albedo** stays within **0.05–0.95** per sRGB channel. **Exempt:** team-channel strokes, UI, and emissive VFX cores during impact frames.
- Shadows are hue-shifted colours, never desaturated grey (§3.3).
- Characters carry a **vertical value gradient**: head, shoulders and weapon lightest, boots darkest ([Valve Dota 2 guide](https://www.cgchannel.com/2012/06/valve-releases-useful-character-design-guide)). Implement it as a world-Z or UV-V gradient in `M_Toon_Char`, ~15–20 % V drop from head to feet [GEN].
- Saturation lives in the albedo and VFX, **not** in post. Global post saturation stays ≤ +15 %.
- **Element colours are fixed** (§4.3 table), following genre conventions: fire is warm, healing green, frost pale blue, poison purple ([RealtimeVFX](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-2-what-are-we-communicating-with-our-vfx/13062)). No blue fire ([Spellbreak](https://www.cgmagonline.com/interviews/spellbreak-art-direction-interview)).

### 4.2 Value hierarchy (greyscale), evaluated by layer

Run on a desaturated gameplay capture ([Game Developer test](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)):

1. **Frames outside impacts:** each hero's upper body is among the highest-contrast elements that are **not VFX**.
2. **Impact frames (2–4):** only VFX primary layers may beat hero contrast. Secondary and tertiary layers never do.
3. **The floor sits in one band**, darker than the heroes' upper bodies. The only exception is the sudden-death zone outside `SafeRadius`, which is desaturated and darkened by ≤ 10 points (§7.9).
4. **Walls and props** are darker or flatter than the floor edges, and never brighter than the heroes.
5. **Telegraph borders** separate from the floor by value alone, and look the same in the sun band and the shadow band (§7.5).

### 4.3 Team colours and reserved hues (per viewer)

| Role | Default | Hex | Relative luminance [GEN calc, WCAG] | Mandatory non-colour cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Self** | Off-white | `#F2F2F2` | 0.89 | Thicker ring + direction notch |
| **Ally** | Sky blue | `#56B4E9` | 0.41 | Solid ring |
| **Enemy** | Vermillion | `#D55E00` | 0.22 | Notched or chevron ring; hazard stripes on telegraphs |

Ally and enemy are Okabe-Ito colours ([palette](https://siegal.bio.nyu.edu/color-palette/)). Orange against blue works for the three most common colour-vision deficiencies ([Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)). Self moved from yellow to off-white so it no longer shares a hue with fire, stun and heal VFX.

**Contrast ratios [GEN calc]:**
- Ally vs enemy ≈ **1.68:1**, well under the 3:1 non-text target ([WCAG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). The shape cue is therefore **mandatory**, not optional.
- Self vs ally ≈ 2.06:1. Self vs enemy ≈ 3.45:1.

**Reserved-hue table** [GEN, OKLCH computed from the hex values]:

| Use | Hex | OKLCH hue | Chroma | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Team: ally | `#56B4E9` | 236° | 0.12 | — |
| Team: enemy | `#D55E00` | 48° | 0.17 | — |
| Team: self | `#F2F2F2` | — | 0.00 | Achromatic; separated by luminance and shape |
| Fire body | `#F5B82E` | 82° | 0.16 | 34° from enemy |
| Fire core | `#FFF0C2` | (92°) | 0.06 | Low chroma, exempt |
| Fire edge / smoke | `#7A5230` | (60°) | 0.07 | Low chroma, exempt. **No saturated red in fire** |
| Energy orbs (centre, death) | `#FFC233` | 83° | 0.16 | Battlerite's orange, shifted to amber |
| Heal | `#7FD14F` | 136° | 0.18 | Gen has no health orb (CharacterGuidelines §4.2) |
| Stun | `#FFE07A` | 93° | 0.13 | — |
| Poison / silence | `#9E7BD9` | 300° | 0.14 | — |
| Frost | `#D7F2FF` | (228°) | 0.03 | Low chroma, exempt. Any saturated frost accent must sit at H ≤ 206° or ≥ 266° |

**Testable rules**
- **Hue separation.** Every saturated (chroma ≥ 0.10) reserved hue sits **≥ 30° in OKLCH hue** from each team hue. Status hues sit ≥ 30° from each other. Element-vs-element and element-vs-orb pairs are exempt, because shape and context separate them.
- **Hero palettes** may not use a hue within 30° of the ally or enemy hue, at chroma ≥ 0.10, as their dominant (largest) saturated colour.
- **Keylines.** Every team-coloured stroke (ring, outline, telegraph border) gets a **1 px keyline**, and the keyline must reach **≥ 3:1 against the floor**. Polarity is set per arena:
  - dark keyline if the floor band's minimum V is ≥ 40 % (black vs V 40 % ≈ 3.7:1);
  - light keyline if its maximum V is ≤ 40 % (white vs V 25 % ≈ 9.3:1);
  - both (double keyline) if the band straddles 40 %.
  - Why it is needed: vermillion alone against a V 40 % floor is only ≈ 1.5:1 [GEN calc].
- **No team or state distinction by colour alone.** Each one also uses shape, pattern, icon or luminance ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)).
- **Team identity never lives on the hero's body or the spell's core.** It goes on dedicated channels: outline, ground ring, health bar, telegraph border, and the projectile ground marker. Hero palettes stay identical for both teams. Overwatch is criticised for weak team readability ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/comparing-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-art-direction)).
- **All team colours come from `DA_TeamColours` / one MPC.** Ship presets on day one: Default, Deuteranopia-friendly, Protanopia-friendly, Tritanopia-friendly and High contrast. Overwatch replaced its colour-blind filter with selectable colours ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/overwatch-gets-updated-colorblind-feature-with-nine-color-options-22546973)).
- Avoid **red against green** for any team or state pair.
- **Known residual conflict:** an enemy fireball's thin team rim (vermillion) barely shows against the amber fire body. For fire, the enemy cue relies on the **ground marker** (chevron + keyline, §7.1 rule 6). Flagged in §12 Q7.

### 4.4 Colour-blind QA

- Add a debug command that cycles `UWidgetBlueprintLibrary::SetColorVisionDeficiencyType` through Deuteranope, Protanope and Tritanope with **CorrectDeficiency = false** (simulation only). In 5.8.3, **Severity is 0–1, where 1 = full simulation**; the engine clamps it and scales it ×10 internally (local `WidgetBlueprintLibrary.cpp:673`).
- Every VFX review passes all three simulations. Simulators surface problems but cannot validate fixes, so recruit colour-blind playtesters before launch ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)).

## 5. Characters

### 5.1 Proportions [GEN, informed by [Didier](https://www.blizzplanet.com/blog/comments/eurogamer_interviews_samwise_didier) and [Riot](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/ask-riot-let-s-talk-clarity/)]

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Height | **5–6 heads** (stocky, heroic) |
| Head (including hair or hat) | 1.2–1.4× realistic; hair or hat is a major silhouette shape |
| Shoulders, pauldrons | Wide; this is where the top-down silhouette is defined |
| Hands, forearms | 1.5× |
| Weapon or signature item | **1.5–2×**; must stick out past the body outline when seen from above |
| Legs | Shorter, darker, simple |
| Minimum feature size | ≥ 5 cm on up-facing surfaces and ≥ 10 cm on vertical ones (≈ 6 px at 1080p, §0) |

Hero size affects how readable the hitbox is ([Ten Ton Hammer](https://www.tentonhammer.com/articles/bloodline-champions-pc-review)). The capsule radius and the visual footprint must agree within ~10 % [GEN].

### 5.2 Primary feature and facing

- Each hero has **one oversized primary feature** that announces its role and survives every skin, unchanged in size and position ([Riot Clarity](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)).
- **Facing must be readable from the top-down silhouette alone.** Only cues that are **≥ 10 cm and asymmetric when seen from above** count:
  - weapon carried on one side;
  - the point of a hood or hat;
  - a single pauldron;
  - a cape or tail trailing behind.
- Head tilt and facial features don't count; at this camera they are 1–3 px.

### 5.3 Shape language per archetype ([RocketBrush](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/shape-language-in-game-character-design-how-to-make-characters-readable-and-consistent))

| Archetype | Body shape (primary / secondary) | Stance | Primary feature examples | VFX shape family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tank | Square / circle | Wide, low centre of gravity | Tower shield, huge hammer | Round domes, rings, walls |
| Bruiser (melee) | Square / triangle | Forward, grounded | Oversized axe, gauntlets | Arcs, ground cracks |
| Ranged DPS | Triangle / square | Upright, side-on | Long bow, rifle, crossbow | Lines, streaks, needles |
| Caster | Triangle / circle | Upright | Staff, floating orb, tome | Orbs, spirals |
| Support | Circle / square | Upright, open | Lantern, censer, wings | Soft wisps, pulses (≤ 60 BPM, [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/the-soul-of-effects-what-is-timing-in-vfx)) |
| Assassin | Triangle | Forward lean | Twin blades, scarf or tail | Sharp slashes, spikes |

### 5.4 Texturing

- **Flat albedo + a per-material gradient + a few painted accents** (highlights, worn edges) ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2797560)). Don't hand-paint lighting into the texture; the ramp does the lighting.
- **No strong baked AO in the albedo.** At most a soft 10–15 % occlusion in deep crevices [GEN].
- Optional cheapest route ([GGXrd](https://www.ggxrd.com/Motomura_Junya_GuiltyGearXrd.pdf)): a **palette atlas** of flat colour swatches, with UV islands snapped to the swatches.
- Roughness ~0.8, constant. Metallic 0, except on accents (buckles, weapon edges).
- Pipeline: sculpt or kitbash → low-poly → bake → quick paint in Substance Painter or 3D-Coat ([Stunlock pipeline](https://blog.stunlock.com/dev-blog-001/)).

### 5.5 Budgets [GEN, low-confidence references]

| Item | Budget | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Hero LOD0, weapon included | **12–25k tris** | Between Dota (~3–4k in-game) and Overwatch (~30k) ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2375439), [UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/polycount-and-performance/413790)) |
| LODs | LOD1 at 50 % triangles, bone-reduced, **Low tier only**. **[GEN]** Set LOD screen sizes explicitly (LOD1 ScreenSize ≈ 0.1 to start), so gameplay renders **LOD0** on Medium and up | The auto-generated sizes (1.0 / 0.5 / 0.25) would render LOD1 in gameplay, where the hero sits at ≈ 0.25 (§8.5) |
| Gameplay texel density | **2.56 px/cm**, the same as the environment ([RebusFarm](https://rebusfarm.net/blog/texel-density-basics-every-artist-should-know)) | On-screen density is only 1.2 px/cm at 1080p and 2.4 at 4K (§0) |
| Texture set | **1× 1024²** by default (BaseColor + packed masks) | A hero with weapon is ~2–3 m² of surface |
| 2048² texture set | Allowed **only if** a hero-select or lobby close-up rig is in scope (§12 Q16), and that is the only reason | — |
| Material slots | ≤ 2 | — |
| Bones | ≤ 120 for the body + ≤ 30 for capes and weapons | — |
| Nanite | **Off** for skinned heroes (overlay/hull workflow, skinning) | — |

Spend polygons on silhouette edges (head, hands, weapon, shoulders), never on interior detail.

### 5.6 Skin rules [GEN, after [Riot Clarity](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/clarity-in-league/) and [Riot VFX updates](https://leagueoflegends.com/en-au/news/dev/dev-behind-the-scenes-of-vfx-updates)]

| Locked on every skin | Free to change |
|---|---|
| Primary feature: silhouette, size, position | Albedo palette, within the §4.1 bands and the reserved-hue rule |
| VFX primary layer: shape, size (= hitbox), timing | VFX secondary and tertiary layers: theme, texture |
| VisualWeight tier and loudness budget | Material accents, trims |
| Team channels and status vocabulary (§7.6) | Emotes, victory poses |
| Capsule and footprint size | Sounds, with the same cast-tell timing |

**No skin may turn up every effect at once**; total loudness stays inside the tier budget. Riot also requires indicators to be exactly the same size on every skin.

### 5.7 Concept workflow [GEN]

1. Paint concepts **over captures from the gameplay camera**, not only as front views ([Didier](https://www.blizzplanet.com/blog/comments/eurogamer_interviews_samwise_didier)).
2. Before modelling, deliver an **8-direction top-down black-silhouette sheet** (45° steps) and a proportion sheet.
3. Put a blockout mesh into PIE (§10 #2 and #2b) **before** sculpting.

## 6. Environment and lighting

### 6.1 Arena ground rules

1. **Symmetrical, quiet fighting floor.** Low-frequency colour shifts, with no high-contrast cracks or tile seams in the central area ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-reduce-visual-confusion-in-your-game)).
2. **Rim budget [GEN].** Spectacle (tribunes, crowds, set pieces) goes on the outer ring, in the Battlerite manner ([GamePressure maps](https://www.gamepressure.com/battlerite/maps/z69616)), but the camera rarely sees it (§0).
   - Cap the rim at **≤ 15 % of environment art time** per arena.
   - Place rim set pieces **within ~6 m outside the play boundary**.
   - Beyond that, use height fog or a flat tone.
3. **Sky:** a simple gradient sky, used only to drive the sky-light colour and ambient.
4. **One strong theme per arena.** Day/night variants are lighting and Curve Atlas row swaps only.
5. **Obstacles are few, large and simple.** **Footprint rule:** the visible base shape seen from above equals the collision shape, within ~10 cm. Use the archetype shape language (§5.3): squares and blocks for cover, round pillars for line-of-sight breakers.
6. **No animated decoration on the floor plane.** Torches, crowds and banners stay on the rim ([Klevestav](https://www.archdaily.com/938441/blizzard-entertainments-philip-klevestav-on-designing-built-environments-in-video-games)).
7. **Foliage only on the rim and in cover clusters.** Grass is short and matches the floor colour through the RVT.
8. **Height fog only**, below and outside the arena (pits, chasms), fading the edges to one tone ([Epic stylised landscape](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/stylized-rendering-landscape?application_version=4.27)). The play area stays fog-free.

### 6.2 Texturing (stylised PBR, not full hand-painting)

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Texel density | **2.56 px/cm** (1024² per 4 m of floor) ([RebusFarm](https://rebusfarm.net/blog/texel-density-basics-every-artist-should-know)); check with the Texture Streaming Accuracy view |
| Library per theme | 3–4 tileables (floor stone, dirt, grass, plaster or wood) + 1–2 trim sheets + a handful of hero props ([Valorant approach](https://inverse.com/gaming/valorant-art-style-interview-moby-francke)) |
| Modular grid | 200 / 400 uu |
| Roughness / metal | Roughness 0.6–1.0; metal only on accents |
| `M_ENV_Master` | Greyscale mask → **Curve Atlas row** gradient ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/curve-atlases-in-unreal-engine-materials)) + world-space macro noise + vertex-colour moss and dirt + RVT tint |
| Material borders | Sharpened world-aligned blend + a coloured outline band ([Epic landscape](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/stylized-rendering-landscape?application_version=4.27)) |
| Ground contact | RVT so grass and prop bases take on the floor colour ([RVT quick start](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/runtimevirtual-texturing-quick-start-in-unreal-engine)) |
| Shapes | Heavy bevels and chamfers; exaggerated forms ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/001agt-crafting-a-stylized-pbr-dagger)) |
| Hand-painting | Only the arena centrepiece, banners and decals |
| Water | Opaque or masked material, depth-fade colour bands, a shoreline foam line, slow panning; no SSR ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/cartoon-water-shader-in-ue4/)) |
| Foliage | Sphere-projected normals (~0.8 mix), 2–3 colour bands, gentle wind on the rim only, with no shadow casting (§3.5) ([Patreon](https://www.patreon.com/posts/25192376)) |
| Draw calls [GEN] | Repeated pieces as **Instanced Static Meshes** (or Packed Level Actors); **≤ 10 unique materials per arena**, mostly MIs of `M_Toon_Env`; merge static clusters that never change |
| Nanite [GEN] | **Not by default.** Arena meshes are low-poly and few, so Nanite's fixed cost may not pay off. Turn it on per mesh only when a measurement shows a gain. If VSM wins §3.6, re-check, because VSM caches Nanite geometry better |
| Mesh distance fields | Only needed for DFAO (config A). Disable **Generate Mesh Distance Fields** on small props that don't need it, to save memory |

### 6.3 Lighting rig (default for every arena)

Settings are [GEN], based on the [80.lv Ghibli village](https://80.lv/articles/making-a-ghibli-inspired-mountain-village-in-unreal-engine-5/) and [Valorant](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/valorant-shaders-and-gameplay-clarity).

| Light | Setting |
|---|---|
| Directional (key) | Movable · warm, 5000–5500 K or a peach tint · pitch 45–55° · yaw 30–45° off the camera axis, so diagonal shadows never hide a hero · Source Angle 0.5 · never animated |
| Sky light | Movable · cool blue or lavender · low intensity · DFAO in config A |
| Fill | None by default; one rect fill only if the A/B test calls for it |
| Shadow shaping | HiddenShadow cards darken the rim and frame the centre |
| Spell lights | ≤ 1 short-lived point light per projectile, radius ≤ 300 uu, alive ≤ 0.3 s after impact, **Cast Shadows off**; global cap per tier in §3.8. Prefer an unlit emissive ground glow (part of the ground marker, §7.1 rule 6), which costs almost nothing, and add a real light only where the glow isn't enough |
| Brightness clamps | Per-area minimum and maximum lit floor value, written in each arena's sheet (Valorant practice) |
| Environment cast shadows | Soft or off near the play area, so they never compete with telegraphs ([Rookies study](https://www.therookies.co/entries/5575)) |

### 6.4 Post-process (one project-wide unbound PPV)

## 7. VFX and telegraphs

### 7.1 Core rules

1. **Footprint equals hitbox.** The primary layer's radius is driven from the same GA/GE radius parameter as the collision, so it can never drift ([Riot VFX updates](https://leagueoflegends.com/en-au/news/dev/dev-behind-the-scenes-of-vfx-updates)). Review with the collision debug-draw on.
2. **Loudness equals threat**, through VisualWeight (§7.2).
3. **Three layers per effect** ([RealtimeVFX Part 1](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-1-creating-areas-of-focus/11614)):

| Layer | Content | Value / saturation | Opacity | Edges |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Hitbox-defining core (projectile head, AoE border) | Widest value range in the effect and the highest saturation, **within the state limits below** | High / opaque | Crisp |
| Secondary | Trails, wisps, theme | Lower | 50–70 % | Softer |
| Tertiary | Sparks, dust | Desaturated, complementary hue | Brief | Small |

   **State limits for the primary core [GEN]:**
   - **In flight or active:** V 75–85 %, S ≤ 85 %, emissive ≤ 1.0× the bloom threshold.
   - **Impact frames (2–4):** may exceed V 85 %, clip to white and bloom, up to the tier caps in §7.2.

4. **One saturated core colour per spell.** Secondary colours are ~⅓ less saturated ([Battlerite VFX critique](https://realtimevfx.com/t/battlerite-fanart-vfx-ver-2/16281)).
5. **Projectiles show direction in a still frame:** bright head at the front, tapered streak behind, stretched along the velocity ([Riot Clarity](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)). **[TASTE #5]** Curffe's projectiles use the Stylized family as their reference look: `NS_ST_Fireball` / `NS_ST_FireballImpact` (Fireball), `NS_ST_GreatFireball` / `NS_ST_GreatFireballImpact` (Great Fireball and Pyroblast), `NS_ST_*_Cast` on the hand. **[TASTE #6]** The live versions add a flame trail to that head: `NS_Curffe_ST_FireballV3` (Fireball, the validated reference) and `NS_Curffe_ST_GreatFireballV3` (Great Fireball and Pyroblast); recipe in §7.4.
6. **Every projectile has a ground marker:** a small, unlit, team-tinted underglow with the team shape cue (solid for ally, chevron for enemy) and a keyline. At a 60° pitch it is the only reliable cue for position, and for fire it carries the enemy cue (§4.3).
7. **Displacements leave a trail** showing the distance travelled ([Dev Blog 003](https://blog.stunlock.com/dev-blog-003/)).
8. **Aimed or charged skills show an aim line or area during the wind-up**, to the caster only, unless the enemy needs it for counterplay.

### 7.2 VisualWeight (per ability design sheet) [GEN, after [Riot](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-2-what-are-we-communicating-with-our-vfx/13062)]

Bands do not overlap. The primary layer always equals the hitbox.

Each spell's tier is set in its champion spec sheet (CharacterGuidelines §7, item 3). The examples below follow `Docs/Design/Champions/Curffe/Curffe.md`. A feedable spell moves up the tiers as it is fed.

| Tier | Weight | Examples | Core on impact | Emissive peak vs bloom threshold | Secondary overhang past the hitbox |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filler | 1–3 | Basic attacks. Curffe: Fireball, `GA_Fireball` (2) | V ≤ 85 %, S ≤ 85 % (never clips) | ≤ 0.8× (never blooms) | 0 % |
| Skillshot | 4–6 | Curffe: Great Fireball at 0–1 flames (5), Meteor Leap (4–6) | May clip for 2 frames | ≤ 1.5× | ≤ 5 % |
| CC / burst | 7–8 | Stuns, knockbacks. Curffe: Great Fireball at 2–3 flames (7–8), Flame Pillar (7–8), Backfire on trigger (7) | May clip for 2–4 frames | ≤ 2× | ≤ 10 % |
| Ultimate | 9–10 | Curffe: Combustion (9–10) | May clip for 2–4 frames | ≤ 3× | ≤ 15 % |

Any overhang must be ≤ 30 % opacity with no crisp edge, so it never reads as the hitbox.

| Tier | Effect screen area cap (1080p; 1 m² ≈ 0.6 %) | Linger after impact | Particles (guide) | Telegraph |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filler | ≤ 1 % | ≤ 0.2 s | ≤ 30 | None |
| Skillshot | ≤ 3 % | ≤ 0.3 s | ≤ 80 | Aim line for the caster only |
| CC / burst | ≤ 8 % | ≤ 0.5 s | ≤ 150 | Ground decal if delayed + audio wind-up |
| Ultimate | ≤ 20 % | ≤ 1.0 s | ≤ 250 | Full telegraph + audio |

If a designed hitbox alone exceeds its tier's area cap, flag it to design. Don't shrink the visual.

**Accepted exception: Curffe's Flame Pillar [TASTE #4].** The CC / burst cap above (≤ 8 %) does not bind Flame Pillar's designed radius, **2 m + 0.5 m per fed flame** (2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.5 m ≈ 7.5 / 11.8 / 17.0 / 23.1 % of the screen [GEN calc, 1 m² ≈ 0.6 %]). The area stays the hitbox; the visual is not shrunk. What keeps it readable: the impact shows its **outer ring first** (frame 1 = the border ring at exactly the hitbox radius, plus a narrow column), the disc fill flashes at ≤ 50 % opacity for 2 frames only, and the telegraph fill (≤ 0.20) is not counted as effect area. Every other §7.2 cap still applies (linger ≤ 0.5 s, ≤ 150 particles, emissive ≤ 2×, overhang ≤ 10 %). Precedence check (§13 step 3): **§3.0**, no conflict (a ring and a column cost less overdraw than a full-disc flash, measured in the packaged build); **§10 #15**, a 3.5 m pillar in the same second as a Combustion eruption is the overlap case the §9.1 flash governor handles, and it must pass the frame-diff check before shipping. Source: `Docs/Design/Champions/Curffe/Curffe-Visuals.md` ⚑ F2.

**Self-buff states are not impact linger.** A state that lasts on the caster (e.g. Combustion's 5 s ablaze state) plays its eruption within the tier caps above, then follows the status rules (§7.6): on the body only, no ground area, never brighter than the in-flight limits of §7.1.

**Per-viewer columns.** Each design sheet has **Self / Ally / Enemy** columns, implemented as GameplayCue variants with a team check.
- Ally effects that pose no threat render at **50–70 % opacity and saturation** on other clients. This follows Riot's per-viewer practice ([Riot](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/clarity-in-league/), [missile pt 2](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-2-what-are-we-communicating-with-our-vfx/13062)).
- Add an "ally VFX intensity" setting [GEN].
- Round-flow events use these variants only when they are team-relevant (§7.9).

**Photosensitivity [GEN].** Impact flashes from every hero **count together** toward the §9.1 flash limit (> 3 flashes in any 1 s over > 25 % of any 10° field fails). An effect that passes alone can fail in a teamfight; the §9.1 flash governor handles the overlap, and §10 #15 checks it.

`GA_GreatFireball` scales from tier 5 to tier 8 with fed flames: each flame gives a **bigger core and a wider value range**, not just more particles. Combustion is Curffe's only 9–10 effect.

### 7.3 Timing

| Phase | Rule [GEN, after [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/the-soul-of-effects-what-is-timing-in-vfx), [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/vfx-staples-shape-color-and-motion/)] |
|---|---|
| Anticipation | **Length = the ability's cast time**, from the CharacterGuidelines §3.1 bands: near-instant 0–0.15 s, short 0.2–0.4 s, medium 0.5–0.9 s, long 1.0–1.6 s (hard CC needs cast + travel ≥ 0.5 s). The cast flash on the hand is only its first 0.1–0.25 s; the pose holds for the rest. A fed cast (Curffe) adds 0.3 s per flame, up to 3 flames (0.15 s per flame while ablaze), shown by the flames leaving the orbit (§7.6). Delayed ground areas show their telegraph **0.6–1.0 s** before impact (CharacterGuidelines §3.1) |
| Impact | **Frame 1 = full-size area flash at the exact radius.** Full intensity for 2–4 frames; this is the only moment at peak value and saturation |
| Dissipation | Drop to ≤ 50 % opacity and saturation within ~0.2 s; total linger per the tier caps in §7.2 |
| Persistent zones | Low-contrast fill (15–30 %) + crisp border; capped count, oldest fades first |
| Stepping (~~signature~~) | **[TASTE #5]: spell VFX are smooth, not stepped.** The rule below now applies only to the round-flow erosion effects of §7.9 (barriers, death puff, shrink wall) and to any future effect the team asks to step. Original rule: quantise the **normalised particle age or the erosion Dynamic Parameter**: `floor(Age*12)/12`. Do this in Niagara or per particle, **not on global `Time`**, which would lock every effect to one shared phase and can hold frame 1 for up to 83 ms. **Impact frame 1 is exempt from stepping.** Persistent zones step their erosion parameter the same way (after [Spellbreak](https://80.lv/articles/spellbreak-developing-a-magic-battle-royale-rpg)) |
| Audio-visual sync | The wind-up sound starts on the anticipation pose and lasts as long as the telegraph. Every dangerous skill has a distinct cast sound, like Jade's Snipe ([Dev Blog 003](https://blog.stunlock.com/dev-blog-003/)) and Riot's audio wind-ups ([Riot Clarity](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)) |

Drive the phases from GameplayCues: OnActive = anticipation, Executed = impact.

### 7.4 Material and Niagara approach: shader-driven first

**Spell VFX reference look [TASTE #5, #9].** **Target: a League of Legends render** [TASTE #9]: stylised and colourful, smooth motion, **soft value gradients** (a warm, not white, core → saturated body → dark edge), one readable shape per effect. **Cel-shading is no longer required** (dropped by the user on 2026-10-09: "je veux juste un rendu à la lol"); existing cel-shaded parts may stay where they read well, new work need not add them. Every spell VFX (Curffe first) is built from the **Stylized family** (`M_ST_*` materials, `NS_ST_*` systems) plus the §7.4 flame recipes: colourful, cartoony and smooth. The `M_VFX_Erode` look (pale flat value bands, 12 fps stepped erosion) was rejected for spell VFX. New spell effects are built in the Stylized style (same material family, sprite and mesh mix, continuous motion), then checked against §7.2 and §10; the conflicts still open are listed in taste-log entry #5. `M_VFX_Erode` below stays the tool for round-flow and environment erosion only.

**Flame recipes [TASTE #6]** (scripted in `Tools/VFX/vfx_recipes.py`, checked with the contact sheets of `Tools/VFX/vfx_capture.py`):
- **Flame trail** (projectiles, leap): the Stylized head + two Vefects ribbons (`NE_Trail`, `MI_VFX_Trail_Fire_01`): a wide orange → red one and a narrow hot one, emitted per second (per-unit spawning emits nothing on a projectile component), recoloured by the `NS_Trail_Fire` colour curve. Big or slow projectiles **taper** the ribbon (width ×1 → ×0.3 over its life); the Vefects default widens it and reads as a solid bar. **One body, not head + trail [TASTE #7, #8]:** the head is not a separate object. A soft round glow (`M_ST_Glow`, warm white) is the front, and a **core ribbon** (`MI_Curffe_FireCoreTrail`: the Vefects trail material on the soft band mask `T_VFX_Gradient_03`) is born under it at the head's diameter, tapers to ×0.12 and runs warm white → yellow → orange → red over its 0.24 s life: that gradient *is* the junction. The flame ribbons are born ≈ 2 × the head diameter wide (their mask fills only the middle half of V) just behind the centre (local space). No hard-edged head parts on top: the opaque orb mesh and the dark-rimmed flame licks are off on the Fireball. **The halo is warm, never white [TASTE #9]:** the glow sprite stays orange (≈ 1.15, 0.4, 0.06) and the pale centre comes only from the core ribbon's birth colour (≈ 2.0, 1.35, 0.45), so the head reads cream-yellow → orange → trail.
- **Flame column / ring** (area impacts, status flames): short bursts of the animated 6×6 flame sheet `MI_FlameShieldFlames` (Mega Magic VFX Bundle), rising, HDR colour (20, 1.9, 0). A column at the centre of Flame Pillar and the Combustion eruption, a ring on Living Flame's border, a looping ring around the body for `State.Countering`.
- Sprites taller than their height above the floor get a position offset, so the floor never cuts them into a straight edge.

**`M_VFX_Erode`** (round-flow and environment erosion; not spell VFX, [TASTE #5]) (Unlit, translucent or masked) ([Vel'Koz fire](https://realtimevfx.com/t/about-fire-from-infernal-velkoz/13491), [Gnomon course](https://www.cgchannel.com/2022/08/tutorial-creating-stylized-vfx-in-unreal-engine-5)):
- R = a painted, **stepped** greyscale shape; flat value layers give a hand-animated look. G = panning noise for UV distortion.
- The erosion threshold comes from a Niagara Dynamic Parameter (stepped per §7.3). Opacity is `SmoothStep(t, t+edgeWidth)`; **edgeWidth ≈ 0 gives cel-hard edges** (default 0.02).
- A second, offset SmoothStep creates an emissive rim band.
- Colour comes from a **Curve Atlas ramp** indexed by the eroded value, giving 2–3 flat bands. For fire, use the §4.3 hues: pale core `#FFF0C2`, amber body `#F5B82E`, desaturated brown edge/smoke `#7A5230`.
- Multiply by particle alpha. Expose a 12 fps step toggle and a team-tint rim parameter.

| Technique | Use for |
|---|---|
| **Mesh + erode shader** (round-flow / environment; spell VFX use the Stylized family, [TASTE #5]) | Slashes, cones, shockwaves, domes, rings. Reusable mesh library: slash arc, cone, hemisphere, ring, tube |
| Small flipbooks (4×4 / 8×4, 512–1024 px) | Smoke puffs, impact stars, hero moments |
| Ribbons (2 layers) | Trails: an alpha-blended dark or desaturated back layer + an additive bright front layer ([Battlerite fan VFX](https://realtimevfx.com/t/battlerite-fanart-vfx-ver-2/16281)) |
| Avoid | Photographic smoke; pure panning without a painted shape, which looks "too rectangular" ([RealtimeVFX](https://realtimevfx.com/t/campfire-using-panning-textures/20165)) |

Large opaque AoE meshes get a **dither fade** where they overlap heroes ([Spellbreak](https://80.lv/articles/spellbreak-developing-a-magic-battle-royale-rpg)).

### 7.8 VFX performance budgets [GEN]

- **Niagara Effect Types.** One per tier (Filler, Skillshot, CC, Ult), with scalability per tier (§3.8) and significance handlers, plus a fifth type, `ET_Env_Ambient`, for ambient environment FX (§7.10). Cull ally cosmetic effects first. **Never cull** enemy CC/ultimate primary layers or telegraphs.
- **Global FX budget [FACT, local source; GEN].** `fx.Budget` is **disabled by default** and must be turned on in the client device profiles before any budget culling works (§7.10). Only `ET_Env_Ambient` uses `bCullByGlobalBudget`; the four gameplay Effect Types keep it off.
- **Overdraw.** ≤ 4 overlapping translucent layers per pixel in typical fights, and ≤ 8 in the §10 #4 clutter scene. Check with the Quad Overdraw and Shader Complexity views ([80.lv: keep overdraw low](https://80.lv/articles/vfx-staples-shape-color-and-motion/)).
- **Dynamic VFX lights.** ≤ 4 alive at once (High tier); the oldest is culled first.
- **Worst case to profile:** 6 heroes firing every ultimate within 2 s, measured with `stat gpu` and `stat particles`.
- **Pooling.** Every system spawned from a GameplayCue uses Niagara component pooling (`Pooling Method = Auto Release`, or a pre-warmed pool for projectiles). This avoids allocation hitches on the game thread.
- **Simulation target.** CPU simulation by default; the particle counts in §7.2 are small. Use GPU simulation only for emitters above ~500 particles, which no tier should need.
- **Fixed bounds** on every system, so Niagara doesn't recompute bounds each frame.
- **Shader cost.** `M_VFX_Erode` stays within ~2 texture samples + simple maths. Check it in the Shader Complexity view; impact flashes may be brief and expensive, persistent zones may not.
- **Prefer opaque or masked to translucent** where the look allows: mesh shockwaves with a dithered or masked erode cost less overdraw than additive sprite stacks.

### 7.9 Round-flow VFX

> **Scope.** This section covers the death, round start, round end, orb and sudden-death events. The **round rules come from CharacterGuidelines §4.2**, which owns them: first to 3 rounds, **no respawn within a round**, rounds of about 90 s, the arena **starts shrinking at 75 s and is fully closed around 120 s**, a **centre orb** from 30 s then every 20 s (+20 energy and a small heal for the team that lands the last hit), and a **death orb** holding half the dead player's energy that only their allies can pick up.

**What Battlerite does** (the sources are thin, so confidence is medium):
- **[FACT]** Patch 1.7 cut the round time from 2:00 to 1:30, so that "the threat of sudden death should become a lot more noticeable than before" ([Stunlock Patch 1.7](https://blog.stunlock.com/patch-1-7)).
- **[FACT]** A round opens with a 3-2-1 countdown. If no team has won after a few minutes, a force field closes in from the edges (sudden death) ([mein-mmo](https://mein-mmo.de/en/battlerite-angespielt,118523/)).
- **[FACT]** The central orb has 50 HP. Breaking it gives the team 25 HP and 25 energy, or 50 energy if only one hero is left. A dead hero drops a health orb that teammates can pick up ([GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/battlerite/maps/z69616)).
- **Unverified:** the orb respawn interval, the look of its spawn timer, and whether each arena themes its own force field ([shapes.inc](https://shapes.inc/fandom/battlerite/maps), low confidence). See §12 Q24.

**What Gen does today [FACT, code]:**
- Heroes respawn after `RespawnDelay = 5.f` (`Source/Gen/Game/GenGameMode.h:40`). `RespawnPlayer` unpossesses and **destroys** the dead pawn, then spawns a fresh one with `RestartPlayer` (`GenGameMode.cpp:101-114`). This is a placeholder: CharacterGuidelines §4.2 has no respawn within a round, so it goes away with the round system. Heroes only spawn at round start.
- `HandleOutOfHealth` (`GenCharacterBase.cpp:322`) sets `State.Dead` as a loose tag with `TagOnly` replication (`:334`) and calls `OnDeathStarted()` on the server (`:337`). On clients, `OnRep_IsDead` calls it instead.
- `OnDeathStarted()` (`:353`) enables ragdoll physics on **every** machine, including the dedicated server (`:362-366`). The fix is in §8.4 #8.
- There is no Gen game state class: the code reads the default `AGameStateBase` (`GenCharacterBase.cpp:54`), so there is no GameState ASC to host world-level GameplayCues.

**How round events reach clients [GEN].** Routing world events through a hero's ASC would tie them to that hero's relevancy and lifetime, so round cosmetics are computed locally from replicated round state:
- Add an `AGenGameState` that replicates the round phase, the phase start time (server world time) and the shrink schedule (start and end radius, start time and duration of each step).
- Each client computes `SafeRadius(t)` from `GetServerWorldTimeSeconds()` with **the same pure function** the server uses for out-of-zone damage (a header-only rule, like `GenFeeding.h` and `GenKnockback.h`).

- **Self / Ally / Enemy variants (§7.2) exist only for team-relevant events:** orb reward motes, death, the death orb, round-start spawn-in and round end. The countdown, orb telegraph, orb spawn and sudden death are team-neutral and have one variant.

**Orb telegraph [GEN].** The orb is a reward that pulls players towards the centre. §7.5 reserves crisp circles and sweeping fills for "get out", so the orb telegraph must never look like an incoming AoE.
- Start 3–5 s before the spawn.
- Draw a **segmented ring** of 6–8 short dashes or rune glyphs. Never draw a continuous crisp border or a filled disc.
- Use energy amber at V 60–75 %.
- Light one segment per elapsed fraction of the wait, so the timer reads as a count, not a sweep.
- No team colour, no team-hue keyline, no chevrons.
- Add a soft rising column of motes at ≤ 30 % opacity.
- Use a non-threat audio cue, such as a chime rather than a wind-up.
- **Test:** freeze a frame 2 s before the spawn. A new player must not say "dodge".

**Death [GEN].**
1. **Death moment.** Flash the body white for 2–4 frames and play a puff sized to the body. A 1.5 m puff covers ≈ 1.8 m², about **1 % of the screen [GEN calc, 1 m² ≈ 0.6 %]**, well under the CC/burst cap of 8 %. It lingers ≤ 0.5 s.
2. **Team channels.** On the same frame, remove the outline (stencil value 0), the team ring and the health bar, so a corpse never reads as a living hero.
3. **Corpse.** Desaturate it to S ≤ 30 % and drop its V by 20 points through `M_Toon_Char` scalar parameters (no new permutation). Then remove it:
   - **Default (cheapest):** once the ragdoll sleeps (§8.4 #8, 2–3 s), play one 12 fps stepped `M_VFX_Erode` puff over the body and hide the mesh under it. No material swap.
   - **Option, only if the team wants a true dissolve:** a masked child master `M_Toon_Char_Death` that shares `M_Toon_Char`'s graph through a material function, with either one `MI_<Hero>_Death` per hero made offline, or, at death, a `UMaterialInstanceDynamic` of that parent filled with `CopyParameterOverrides(<hero MI>)` (local `MaterialInstanceDynamic.h:194`). One shared MI cannot work, because each hero's MI carries its own textures and gradients. This depends on the Toon BSDF working on masked materials (§12 Q13). Count shader permutations before and after.
   - Low tier: no dissolve; hide the corpse at 2 s.
   - With no respawn within a round (CharacterGuidelines §4.2), the corpse stays until the round ends, so this removal step is **required**. (The placeholder 5 s respawn destroys the pawn sooner, `GenGameMode.cpp:110-111`.)
4. **Trigger.** Fire the cue locally from the cosmetic death path (`OnDeathStarted`, reached through `OnRep_IsDead` on clients), not from the `State.Dead` loose tag. **[FACT, code]** The tag is added `TagOnly`, and loose tags do not trigger GameplayCues on their own.
5. **Ragdoll.** Clients only (§8.4 #8).

**Dead player's view [GEN].** A dead hero stays out for the rest of the round (CharacterGuidelines §4.2).
- On your own death, run a desaturation pulse of 0.8–1.0 s on the **local camera only**. Use the camera component's post-process settings with a blend weight (Colour Grading saturation). **[INFERENCE]** Colour grading is folded into the tonemapper, so this adds no pass, and it does not break §6.4's single project-wide PPV.
- Then spectate a living teammate **in full colour**, with a "Spectating <name>" banner.
- Never leave the screen grey. Riot names the "gray screen" after a lost teamfight as a clarity problem ([Riot, Summoner's Rift update](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2014/06/update-to-summoners-rift-headed-to-pbe.html)).

**Sudden death [GEN].**
- **Desaturate the floor, darken it only a little, never add fog.** One MPC, `MPC_Round`, holds `SafeCentre`, `SafeRadius` and `ShrinkAlpha`, written each frame on the client from the `AGenGameState` function above. Outside the radius, `M_ENV_Master` cuts floor saturation by about 50 % and drops V by **≤ 10 points**, clamped so albedo stays ≥ 0.05 (§4.1). This costs a few ALU ops in an opaque pass that already exists.
  - This makes a second floor band during sudden death only. It is the one declared exception to §4.2 rule 3.
  - **[INFERENCE, unmeasured]** A translucent or volumetric fog over a large share of the screen would likely cost 0.3–1 ms of overdraw at 1080p. It would also break the §7.8 limit of ≤ 4 (or ≤ 8) overlapping translucent layers. Rejected under §3.0.
- **Keylines follow the zone.** Darkening can move the floor across the §4.3 keyline polarity threshold (a 40–55 % band darkened by 20 points would leave a dark keyline at ≈ 1.7:1). So `M_VFX_Telegraph`, the team-ring material and the projectile ground-marker material read the same `MPC_Round` and switch to the **double keyline** (§4.3) outside `SafeRadius`. **[GEN calc]** Black or white always reaches ≥ 4.5:1 against any floor luminance, so a double keyline passes everywhere. It is a lerp on an existing parameter, with no new static switch. Test: §10 #14.
- **Drawn radius vs damage radius.** Draw the wall and the preview line about `RTT/2 × shrink speed` **inside** the computed `SafeRadius` (from the local player's measured ping), so the drawn line never shows more safe ground than the server applies (§7.5). Test: with `NetEmulation.PktLag 150`, a hero standing exactly on the drawn line takes no damage (§10 #14).
- **Boundary wall.** One unlit **masked** cylinder or ribbon mesh at the drawn radius, so it adds no translucent overdraw.
  - **Height 0.3–0.4 m.** At a −60° pitch, a wall of height h on the camera-near side hides a hero standing up to h / tan 60° behind it (a 1 m wall hides most of a hero within 0.58 m). At 0.4 m it covers the feet only, and the stencil outline and occluded fill (§3.4) draw over it.
  - Crisp top edge of 3–4 px, with the double keyline.
  - The wall erodes in 12 fps steps.
  - **Screen-area cap [GEN calc].** Area ≈ 2π · r · 113 px/m × h · 60.5 px/m, out of 2.07 M px at 1080p (113 px/m averages the 121 and 105 px/m floor scales of §0; 60.5 px/m is the vertical scale, cos 60° × 121). At a final radius r = 5 m: h = 0.4 m ≈ **4 %**; h = 1 m ≈ 10 %. The shrink runs to fully closed (CharacterGuidelines §4.2), so the worst case is r ≈ 5 m, where the whole ring is on screen; bigger rings leave the screen and smaller ones are shorter. Keep it ≤ 5 % there, and verify with a capture around 110 s (§10 #14).
- **Hazard identity is a shape.** Draw a slow, low-contrast diagonal hatch on the wall only.
  - **[FACT]** The Game Accessibility Guidelines warn against moving repeated patterns over ≥ 25 % of the screen ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-flickering-images-and-repetitive-patterns/)). So keep ≤ 5 visible stripes, each scroll cycle lasting ≥ 1 s.
  - The hue must sit ≥ 30° in OKLCH from every team and status hue (§4.3). It is undecided (§12 Q25).
  - Each arena may add a theme tint on the secondary layer only.
- **Preview.** The shrink is continuous from 75 s to ~120 s, so the wall itself is the edge and there is no separate preview line. If design later makes the shrink stepped, a dashed preview line shows where each step stops, and it never shows less travel than the real shrink (§7.5).
- **Damage outside the zone.**
  - On your own screen: a **sustained** edge ramp that rises while you stand outside and falls when you return (§9.1). Never a pulse per damage tick.
  - The victim hit flash (§8) fires **at most 2 times per second**, however fast the damage ticks.
  - No world VFX per tick.

**Match victory.** UI plus a staged camera (§12 Q16). Any in-world celebration VFX stay within the Ultimate caps (§7.2) and add no dynamic lights.

### 7.10 Ambient environment VFX

> "When fights break out, the environment should be the background" ([Riot, Summoner's Rift update](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2014/06/update-to-summoners-rift-headed-to-pbe.html)).

Ambient FX are the first thing culled under load. Gameplay FX are never culled to make room for them.

**Engine facts [FACT, local 5.8.3 source]:**
- **Effect Type options** (`Engine/Plugins/FX/Niagara/Source/Niagara/Classes/NiagaraEffectType.h`):
  - `UpdateFrequency`: SpawnOnly, Low (1.0 s), Medium (0.5 s), High (0.25 s) or Continuous.
  - `ENiagaraCullReaction`: Kill, Kill and Clear, Asleep (resumes when the cull tests pass again), Asleep and Clear, or Pause.
  - Visibility culling: `bCullByViewFrustum` and `bAllowPreCullingByViewFrustum` ("cull off screen systems before they even spawn"; needs fixed bounds), `MaxTimeOutsideViewFrustum`, `bCullWhenNotRendered`.
  - Scalability: `bCullByDistance` / `MaxDistance`, `MaxInstances`, `MaxSystemInstances`, `bCullByGlobalBudget` / `MaxGlobalBudgetUsage`.
  - Significance handlers: Distance or Age.
- **The global FX budget is off by default.**
  - `FFXBudget::bEnabled = false` (local `Engine/Source/Runtime/Engine/Private/Particles/FXBudget.cpp:285`).
  - `fx.Budget.GameThread`, `fx.Budget.GameThreadConcurrent` and `fx.Budget.RenderThread` all default to 2.0 ms.
  - `fx.Budget.EnabledInEditor` defaults to false.
  - Budget tracking is compiled out of server builds (`WITH_GLOBAL_RUNTIME_FX_BUDGET` = `!UE_SERVER`).
  - **So `bCullByGlobalBudget` does nothing until the budget is turned on.**
- Epic's guidance: give every system an Effect Type, and use fewer emitters to cut fixed CPU overhead ([Niagara scalability](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/scalability-and-best-practices-for-niagara)).

**Visual rules for `NS_ENV_*` [GEN].** All are testable with §10 #1 and #7.
1. **Flames** on torches and braziers:

2. **No light pooling or ground glow on the play floor from ambient sources.** Ground glow is reserved for projectile markers (§7.1 rule 6). 6. **Crowds.**
   - Default: instanced static meshes or cards, with a WPO bob, on the rim.

   - Crowds react only to round end (§7.9).
7. **Greyscale test (§10 #1):** no ambient element beats any hero in contrast.

**Hit-stop: cosmetic only, applied on each client**

Hit-stop must **never touch `CustomTimeDilation` or world time dilation**. `CustomTimeDilation` slows the whole actor, including `CharacterMovementComponent`. Evidence in the local 5.8 source:
- `ActorComponent.cpp` `ExecuteTickHelper` passes dilated time to components.
- `Actor.cpp:379` uses `DeltaTime*CustomTimeDilation`.
- `CharacterMovementComponent.cpp:9453/12821/13253` stores it in saved moves and uses it for server deltas.

Using it for a local-only hit-stop would cause prediction mismatches and server corrections.

How to do it:
- **Mechanism.** On each client, locally, set `USkeletalMeshComponent::GlobalAnimRateScale` to ≈ 0 (or `bPauseAnims`) on the attacker's and victim's meshes for N ms, then restore it. Pause their attached Niagara components with `SetPaused(true)` for the same N ms. The server never applies it.
- **Caveats [FACT, source; details in §8.4 #6].**
  - **Root-motion montages.** Pausing the animation also pauses root-motion extraction, and the server rejects moves that are ahead of a paused mesh, so it pops for **all viewers**, not only the autonomous proxy. Skip hit-stop on meshes playing root-motion montages, or freeze only a non-root-motion layer. Dashes use Root Motion Sources (§8.4 #5), which hit-stop can't stall, so most dash abilities avoid this caveat altogether.
  - **Replicated montages.** On meshes playing a replicated montage, cap freezes at **≤ 90 ms**, or use a catch-up rate (§8.4 #6). The 120 ms hard cap below still applies everywhere.
  - **Hit-reacts** go in a separate `HitReact` slot group, so they never stop the ability's montage.
- **No hit-stop on CC or wind-ups.** Never freeze a victim on a hit that applies CC or knockback: the CC pose must start with its tag, and stuns can be as short as 0.5 s (CharacterGuidelines §3.2). Never freeze a mesh during its anticipation pose: cast time is the telegraph (CharacterGuidelines §3.1).
- **Durations.** Basic projectile ≈ 0 ms · heavy melee 40–60 ms · ultimate or kill 80–120 ms. **The 120 ms cap is hard:** long freezes give a third player a free hit in 3v3 ([Sakurai](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)).

### 8.1 Locomotion

**What the code sets [FACT, `Source/Gen/Character/GenCharacterBase.cpp`]:**
- `MaxWalkSpeed = 550` (`:32`); the `MoveSpeed` attribute updates it at runtime (`:223`, `:319`).
- `MaxAcceleration = 4096` (`:33`); braking deceleration 4096 (`:34`) and ground friction 8 (`:35`).
- `RotationRate` yaw 1080°/s (`:31`), with `bOrientRotationToMovement` (`:30`).
- While casting, `SetFaceAim` (`:110`) switches to controller-desired rotation.
- `UGenGA_Projectile::ApplyCastSlow` applies a `CastMoveSpeedMultiplier` slow while casting. So heroes **can move while casting**, at reduced speed, as CharacterGuidelines §3.1 requires.

**What that means for animation [GEN calc]:**
- 0 → 550 uu/s takes 550 / 4096 ≈ **0.134 s (~4 frames at 30 fps)**. Braking takes about the same.
- A 180° reversal takes 180 / 1080 ≈ **0.17 s (5 frames)**.
- **Consequence:** start, stop and pivot clips have almost no time to play. Outside casts the capsule always faces its movement direction, so idle turn-in-place never happens. Strafing only exists while casting.

### 8.2 Rig and pipeline

**Skeleton [GEN].**
- **Every hero uses one shared skeleton:** the UE5 `SK_Mannequin` hierarchy, bone names and bone order.
  - The project is already mannequin-oriented: the mesh is rotated −90° yaw "like the UE5 mannequin" (`GenCharacterBase.cpp:40`), and `Content/Characters/Mannequins` holds the template anims.
- **Stylised proportions** (1.2–1.4× head, 1.5× hands, short legs; §5.1) go in the **mesh and its reference pose**, never in a new hierarchy.
- Extra leaf bones for capes, hair and weapons are allowed, within the §5.5 limit of ≤ 30.
- Drop twist and corrective bones that the stylised mesh doesn't skin to, so the body stays ≤ 120 bones (§5.5). The current count is still to be measured (§12 Q38).
- **[FACT]** Meshes that share a hierarchy, bone names and bone order can share one Skeleton asset. Compatible Skeletons cover near-identical hierarchies ([Epic skeletons](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/skeletons-in-unreal-engine)). Use Compatible Skeletons only if a hero truly needs a different hierarchy, such as a tail.

**Retargeting: IK Retargeter, baked offline [FACT, [IK Retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/ik-rig-animation-retargeting-in-unreal-engine), [Auto Retargeting](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/auto-retargeting-in-unreal-engine)]:**
1. Create one IK Rig per source skeleton (UEFN_Mannequin/GASP, Paragon, Quaternius, Mixamo).
2. Create one IK Retargeter per source → Gen pair, with **hand and foot IK goals on**. That keeps chunky arms out of the torso and keeps feet planted on short legs.
3. **Bake** with Export Selected Animations into `Content/Gen/Animations/<Source>/`. Batch export needs a **saved** retargeter asset, not an auto-generated one.
4. **Never use runtime `Retarget Pose From Mesh`.** Epic doesn't document its cost, and it gains nothing visible here. Baked clips cost 0 at runtime.

**Animation Blueprint structure: the Lyra pattern [FACT, [Lyra animation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-in-lyra-sample-game-in-unreal-engine); [Anim BP linking](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-blueprint-linking-in-unreal-engine)]:**

**Rules [GEN]:**
- **`ChargeMontage` length = the GA's `CastTime`.**
  - **[FACT, code]** `GenGA_Projectile` already times the release with `UAbilityTask_WaitDelay(CastTime)` in `StartCasting`, and spawns the projectile right after `CastMontage` starts in `OnTargetDataReady`. ### 8.4 Networking (GAS montages, root motion, death)

Code references to `GenGA_Projectile.cpp` name functions, not line numbers, because that file changes often.

Sources: the local 5.8.3 GameplayAbilities plugin, `Character.cpp` and the CMC; [tranek GASDocumentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tranek/GASDocumentation/master/README.md); Epic docs. GAS line numbers below marked ~ are approximate, as given by the source reading.

**1. How montages replicate [FACT, `AbilitySystemComponent_Abilities.cpp`, `PlayMontageInternal` ~L3041, `OnRep_ReplicatedAnimMontage`]:**
- Other players see a montage **only** if it is played through the ASC (`PlayMontageAndWait` / `ASC::PlayMontage`). The ASC then fills the push-model `RepAnimMontageInfo` (`AbilitySystemComponent.cpp` ~L1855) and calls `ForceNetUpdate()`.
- A plain `AnimInstance->Montage_Play`, or the Blueprint "Play Montage" node, does **not** replicate (tranek §9.3).
- The autonomous (owning) client ignores `RepAnimMontageInfo`: it relies on its own predicted montage.
- **Simulated proxies start every montage at position 0.** They jump to the server position only when the error is > 0.1 s (`MONTAGE_REP_POS_ERR_THRESH`). When they jump, they fire every notify they skipped, in one frame.
- The ASC tracks **one** replicated montage at a time.
- **Rules [GEN]:**
  - Every montage other players must see goes through `UAbilityTask_PlayMontageAndWait`, as `ChargeMontage` and `CastMontage` already do.
  - No combo trees built on section jumps; use one montage per step.
  - Debug with `net.Montage.Debug 1`.

**2. Misprediction: a real gap in `GenGA_Projectile` [FACT, source + code]:**
- A predicted montage is auto-stopped (0.25 s fade) **only** when the server rejects the **activation** key (`ClientActivateAbilityFailed_Implementation` ~L2279).
- `CastMontage` starts later, in `OnTargetDataReady`. A commit failure there rejects no prediction key, so `OnPredictiveMontageRejected` never fires.
- The client ends its own ability right after playing the montage, without replicating the end (at the end of `OnTargetDataReady`). When the server's end or cancel arrives, `RemoteEndOrCancelAbility` returns early because the spec is no longer active (local `AbilitySystemComponent_Abilities.cpp:2166`). The montage task is already gone, and it was created with `bStopWhenAbilityEnds = false`.
- As noted in #1, the owning client also ignores `RepAnimMontageInfo`.
- **Failure case:** if the server's target data is invalid or its `CommitAbility` fails (the two `EndAbility(..., true, true)` branches of `OnTargetDataReady`), the client plays the whole throw and no fireball ever appears. Replacing the server's `EndAbility(..., true, true)` with `CancelAbility` would **not** fix this, for the reasons above.
- **Fix [GEN, code]:** in both server failure branches, call a Client RPC on `AGenCharacterBase`, e.g. `ClientStopCastMontage(UAnimMontage* Montage)`, which calls `AnimInstance->Montage_Stop(0.25f, Montage)` if that montage is still playing. Simulated proxies need nothing: the server never played `CastMontage`, so they never saw it.
- The stun path is already correct: `OnCastInterrupted` → `CancelAbility` runs while the GA is still active on both sides, so the task's cancel binding stops the montage.
- Keep `bStopWhenAbilityEnds = false` for the normal case, where the throw continues after the GA ends.
- **Test (added to the #9 pass):** force `CommitAbility` to fail on the server only (e.g. drain energy server-side with a cheat) and check that the client's throw is cut within 1 RTT.

**3. Timing: what each player sees [INFERENCE, from source + tranek §4.10.3]:**
- **Opponents** see the montage and the replicated projectile with the same delay, so the anticipation pose (the §8 telegraph) stays consistent with the fireball.
- **The caster** sees their fireball about **1 RTT** after their own throw frame. GAS does not predict spawned actors.
- **Rules [GEN]:**
  - Keep the server-authoritative spawn from `Avatar->GetActorLocation()` (`UGenGA_Projectile::SpawnProjectile`). Sockets are cosmetic, and the server doesn't refresh bones (§8.5).
  - If playtests at ≥ ~80 ms RTT show a visible gap, add a **caster-only cosmetic fake projectile** (the Unreal Tournament approach), one pooled Niagara system, later and not in v0 (§12 Q35).

**4. What drives damage timing [FACT, `AbilitySystemBlueprintLibrary.cpp` `SendGameplayEventToActor` ~L82-105; `AbilitySystemPrivate.h`]:**
- In 5.8, `SendGameplayEventToActor` opens a prediction window only if `AbilitySystem.Fix.AllowPredictiveGEFlags` allows it, and the default is 0. So **damage driven by a notify is server-only.**
- `bTriggerOnDedicatedServer` defaults to true (`AnimTypes.h:383`).
- Epic describes Branching Point as "precise, synchronous, higher-cost" ([notifies](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-notifies-in-unreal-engine)).

| Ability kind | Timing source [GEN] |
|---|---|
| Projectiles (current `GenGA_Projectile`) | **GA timer** (`WaitDelay(CastTime)`), as today. No notify. The montage is authored to match it (§8.3). The release never depends on server animation ticking |
| Melee / hitbox abilities (future) | A **montage-track** native notify sends a GameplayEvent (e.g. `Event.Ability.Release`). Start `WaitGameplayEvent` **before** playing the montage: it does not replay events sent before it started listening. Arm a fallback `WaitDelay` (authored action time + ~0.1 s) **only** when the montage task reports `OnInterrupted` or `OnBlendOut` before the event has arrived. A single `bReleased` guard in the GA makes the release fire exactly once. **Only the server** applies the GE or spawns the hitbox; clients use the frame for cosmetics only. Test: under `NetEmulation.PktLoss 10`, the server log shows exactly one release per activation |
| Cosmetics on the action frame | Local cues (`AnimNotify_GameplayCue`, which calls `ExecuteGameplayCue_NonReplicated`), so no multicast RPC (tranek §4.8.3) |

**5. Dashes, leaps, knockbacks: Root Motion Sources [FACT, [CMC networking](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/understanding-networked-movement-in-the-character-movement-component-for-unreal-engine); `AbilityTask_ApplyRootMotion_Base.cpp` sets `bSimulatedTask = true`]:**
- **Default [GEN]:** a LocalPredicted GA with an **in-place** cosmetic montage, plus one of:
  - `ApplyRootMotionConstantForce` (dash);
  - `ApplyRootMotionJumpForce` (leap);
  - `ApplyRootMotionMoveToForce` (blink or charge to a fixed endpoint).
- If an authored clip has root motion, pass `AnimRootMotionTranslationScale = 0`.
- `FinishVelocityMode = ClampVelocity` (about `MaxWalkSpeed`) or `SetVelocity`, so there is no slide after the dash.
- Give each dash a **unique ForceName**, and keep durations of 0.15–0.3 s.
- Drive distance and duration from the same GA variables as the hitbox and the §7.1 rule 7 trail.
- **Why:** RMS doesn't depend on animation playback, so hit-stop can't stall it. Its params replicate, so simulated proxies run the same motion.
- **Current code [FACT]:** knockbacks don't use RMS yet. `AGenCharacterBase::ApplyKnockback` calls `LaunchCharacter` on the server, plus a `ClientApplyKnockback` RPC to the owner. Migrating them to `ApplyRootMotionMoveToForce` is planned, not done. Knocked-back meshes get no hit-stop anyway (§8).
- **Known issue:** UE-206616 (RMS dash teleports forward and back under packet loss) was read only through search snippets, and its fix status in 5.8.3 is unverified (§12 Q37).
- Reserve **root-motion montages** for rare authored lunges, and never hit-stop them.
- Stay on `ACharacter` + CMC. **[FACT]** Mover is still `IsExperimentalVersion: true` in 5.8.3 (local `Engine/Plugins/Experimental/Mover/Mover.uplugin`).

**6. Hit-stop vs montage replication [FACT, source; extends §8]:**
- On a **simulated proxy**, freezing the mesh leaves its montage behind the server by the length of the freeze, and play rate 1 never catches up. Freezes ≥ ~100 ms pass the 0.1 s threshold: the proxy **snaps and burst-fires the notifies it skipped**. Shorter freezes leave a lag until the montage ends.
- **Root-motion montages:** `ACharacter::CanUseRootMotionRepMove` (`Character.cpp` ~L1892) rejects server moves that are ahead of the client. A paused proxy pops on **every** viewer, not only on the owner.
- `GlobalAnimRateScale` scales only the AnimInstance update (`SkeletalMeshComponent.cpp` ~L1765-1784). The freeze stays purely visual, which confirms the §8 mechanism.
- `Montage_Play` stops every montage in the same **group** (`AnimInstance.cpp` ~L2770). A local hit-react in `DefaultGroup` would interrupt the GA's montage on the owning client.

**Rules [GEN]:**
- **Montage freezes.** Default: on a mesh playing a replicated montage, cap the freeze at **≤ 90 ms**. Option, settled by A/B test (§12 Q34): keep the full freeze, then catch up with `GlobalAnimRateScale` chosen so that **(rate − 1) × catch-up duration = freeze length** (e.g. 2.0 for 1× the freeze, or 1.5 for 2×). Catch up **only during follow-through**, never while the montage is in an anticipation section: a sped-up anticipation shortens the telegraph opponents read (§8). The 120 ms hard cap in §8 still applies everywhere.
- **Root-motion montages.** Skip hit-stop on meshes playing root-motion montages, **for all viewers**.
- **Hit-reacts.** Put hit-reacts in a **separate `HitReact` slot group** (additive, upper body), or drive them from an AnimBP additive.
- **Death.** Restore `GlobalAnimRateScale = 1` before the ragdoll starts.

**7. CC loops come from tags, not montages [FACT, `GameplayEffectTypes.h` `FGameplayTagBlueprintPropertyMapping` ~L1427; Lyra docs; code]:**
- **[FACT, code]** Gen has no C++ AnimInstance class, and the Gameplay Tag Property Map is not available on a plain `UAnimInstance` (Lyra exposes it through its own C++ `ULyraAnimInstance`).
- **Add `UGenAnimInstance` [GEN, code]:** a C++ AnimInstance with a `UPROPERTY` `FGameplayTagBlueprintPropertyMap`, initialised with the ASC from `AGenCharacterBase::OnAbilitySystemInitialized` (`GenCharacterBase.cpp:214`). That function also runs on clients, where the ASC comes from the PlayerState. Reparent `ABP_Champion` to it.
- Fill the map in the ABP Class Defaults: e.g. `bIsStunned` ← `State.Stunned`, `bIsIncapacitated` ← `State.Incapacitated`, `bIsFeared` ← `State.Feared`, `bIsAirborne` ← `State.Airborne`. The CC states then start and end exactly with the tag, as §8 requires, and don't compete for the one replicated montage the ASC tracks.
- **CC beats any montage still playing.** `CastMontage` keeps playing after the GA ends (`bStopWhenAbilityEnds = false`), so a stun landing during the follow-through finds no active GA to cancel, and the slot would override the stun pose. Place the CC states **after** the montage slots in the AnimGraph (Blend Poses by bool on the CC flags, 0–2 frame blend), so the CC pose wins on every viewer on the frame the tag arrives. A server-side `CurrentMontageStop()` alone would not reach the owning client, which ignores `RepAnimMontageInfo`.
- While the GA is still active, the existing stun path (`WaitGameplayTagAdded` on `State_Stunned` → `CancelAbility`) also stops the cast.
- Test: stun a hero 0.1 s after release; the stun pose appears on the same frame as the tag for all viewers.
- Perf: one bool per tag change, read on the worker thread.

**8. Death and ragdoll [FACT, code: `GenCharacterBase.cpp:322`, `:353-369`, `GenGameMode.cpp:101-114`; engine: `SkeletalMeshComponentPhysics.cpp:364`, `PhysicsSettingsCore.cpp:47`]:**
- The dedicated server currently simulates the ragdoll (`OnDeathStarted`, `:362-366`).
- The engine guard skips this only when `bSimulateSkeletalMeshOnDedicatedServer` is false, and that setting defaults to **true**.
- The guard tests `IsRunningDedicatedServer()`, which is false in PIE, so PIE never shows the problem.
- Respawn spawns a **fresh pawn**: `RespawnPlayer` unpossesses and destroys the dead one, then calls `RestartPlayer` (`GenGameMode.cpp:110-114`).

**Rules [GEN, code]:**
1. **Guard the ragdoll.** In `OnDeathStarted`, wrap the ragdoll in `if (bRagdollOnDeath && GetNetMode() != NM_DedicatedServer && GetMesh()->GetPhysicsAsset())`. Keep the movement shutdown and capsule collision change on the server.
2. **Optionally** set Project Settings → Physics → Simulate Skeletal Mesh On Dedicated Server = false, once you have checked that nothing on the server needs simulated skeletal bodies.
3. **Replicate a small death payload** next to `bIsDead`: the killing-blow direction (`FVector_NetQuantizeNormal`) and its magnitude (`uint8`). Each client applies the same impulse, so poses look similar everywhere. **Never replicate ragdoll bone poses**; one forum estimate puts that at about 40 kB/s per 22-body ragdoll ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/questions/79791/replication-of-a-physics-ragdoll.html)).
4. **On clients, bound the cost until the pawn goes away.** After 2–3 s, call `PutAllRigidBodiesToSleep()`, or freeze the pose. Then remove the corpse per §7.9. The corpse actor is destroyed at round end (no respawn within a round, CharacterGuidelines §4.2; the placeholder respawn destroys it sooner), so no physics or collision needs restoring.
5. **Respawn needs no reset path today.** Restoring physics, collision and the mesh attachment on the same actor is only needed if §12 Q23 later chooses pawn pooling.
6. Whether to play a short death montage before the ragdoll, and whether Low drops the ragdoll, is a taste call. Log it in §13 (§12 Q32).

**9. Smoothing and test pass [FACT, `CharacterMovementComponent.cpp` ~L712-718 and `:716-717`; [network emulation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-network-emulation-in-unreal-engine)]:**

- **Blinks.** A server-side `TeleportTo` carries no teleport flag to simulated proxies. Proxies snap only when the jump is longer than 384 uu. Between 256 and 384 uu the error is clamped to 256 uu and smoothed over 0.1 s; under 256 uu it is fully smoothed. **[GEN calc]** A 300 uu blink therefore smears ≈ 256 cm × 1.21 px/cm ≈ 310 px across the screen in 0.1 s.
- **Rule [GEN, code]:** blinks are either **≥ 400 uu**, or they replicate a blink counter whose OnRep on simulated proxies zeroes `MeshTranslationOffset` and `OriginalMeshTranslationOffset` in `FNetworkPredictionData_Client_Character` (local `CharacterMovementComponent.h:3187-3190`), so the proxy snaps.
- **Network test pass [GEN].** Run it for every new movement or montage ability, in PIE with 2 clients + a dedicated server:
  1. Average profile.
  2. Bad profile with `NetEmulation.PktLoss 10`.
  3. Both runs with `p.NetShowCorrections 1` and `net.Montage.Debug 1`.
  4. Checks: no montage snaps or burst-fired notifies during hit-stop; a 300 uu blink snaps on the other client (no smear); client 1's lean shows on client 2 (§8.1); a server-only commit failure cuts the client's throw within 1 RTT (#2); a stun during follow-through shows the stun pose on every viewer (#7).

### 8.5 Animation performance (clients and dedicated server)

**The dedicated server runs animation on one thread [FACT, local source]:**
- `FApp::ShouldUseThreadingForPerformance()` returns false when `IsRunningDedicatedServer()` (`Core/Private/Misc/App.cpp:290-302`).
- Parallel anim update and evaluation are gated on that call (`SkeletalMeshComponent.cpp:2915`, `:3132`).
- So every hero's anim work lands on the server game thread.
- In-editor PIE does **not** reproduce this, and it skips engine guards that test `IsRunningDedicatedServer()` (`Core/Public/Misc/CoreMisc.h:152-168`).
- **Rules [GEN]:**
  - Write Gen's server-only branches with `GetNetMode() == NM_DedicatedServer`, which also works in PIE.
  - Take server CPU numbers only from a `-server` process, never from PIE. **v0 route:** the editor binary (`UnrealEditor.exe Gen.uproject L_Arena -server -log -trace=cpu,frame`). It carries editor overhead and uncooked assets, so treat its numbers as an **upper bound**.
  - A packaged server target needs a source-built engine; the installed launcher 5.8.3 used on this machine can't build Server targets. It becomes the measurement route only if §12 Q1 settles on a source build.

**`VisibilityBasedAnimTickOption` on a dedicated server [FACT, local source].** The enum is in `SkinnedMeshComponent.h:96-116`. A dedicated server never renders, so `bRecentlyRendered` stays false (`SkinnedMeshComponent.cpp:1864`).

**Decision [GEN]:**
- **v0: heroes keep `AlwaysTickPose` on the dedicated server.**

  - What it costs: the graph update for 6 heroes on one server thread, but **no** pose evaluation, so no Control Rig or skinning.
  - **Never read bone or socket transforms for gameplay on the server.** Use the actor or capsule transform plus authored offsets, as `SpawnProjectile` already does.
- **Phase 2:** switch the server to `OnlyTickMontagesWhenNotRendered`, set at runtime in `PostInitializeComponents` when `GetNetMode() == NM_DedicatedServer`. Do it **only when all four gates pass**:
  1. Every gameplay notify sits on a **montage** notify track (rule in §8.2); the §10 #16 audit passes.
  2. PIE with 2 clients + a dedicated server, with a log in each notify's `Notify()` that checks `HasAuthority()`, shows every montage-track notify and every `PlayMontageAndWait` end firing on the server. Add one test notify inside a sequence to confirm it is dropped.
  3. Re-test every montage with `AnimRootMotionTranslationScale ≠ 1`. UE-211293 (scale applied after a reset on the last tick in this mode) is unverified for 5.8.3.
  4. A `-server` Insights capture shows the hero anim update is worth the saving (§12 Q30).

- **Clients keep `AlwaysTickPose`.**

**Photosensitivity [FACT, [WCAG 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html), [GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-flickering-images-and-repetitive-patterns/)]:**
- **The rule:** no more than 3 flashes in any 1 s, unless the flash stays under the general and red thresholds.
  - A general flash is a pair of opposing changes of ≥ 10 % relative luminance, where the darker state is below 0.80.
  - Any opposing transition involving saturated red counts as a red flash.
  - The area threshold is 25 % of **any 10° visual field** (0.006 sr). WCAG approximates that field as a **341 × 256 px** rectangle on a 1024 × 768 screen.
- **GAG also says:** avoid flashing sequences longer than 5 s; name options by what they control (never "epilepsy safe"); run the Harding test.

**Rules for Gen [GEN]:**
- **Working box [GEN calc].** At 1080p on a 24″ monitor seen from ~60 cm, 10° is ≈ 380 px wide, so WCAG's **341 × 256 px** box is the conservative choice; use it. 25 % of it is ≈ 21 800 px, about **1 % of a 1080p screen**, not 25 % of the screen. Every CC/burst (≤ 8 %) and Ultimate (≤ 20 %) impact flash that clips to white exceeds it, so each one counts as a flash.
- **Clutter check (§10 #15).** In no 1 s window may any 341 × 256 px box contain > 3 general flashes, or any red flash, over > 25 % of its area. Impact frames (§7.3) from **all** heroes count together (§7.2). Test with a frame-diff script on a captured 1080p video of the §10 #4 clutter scene, or with PEAT or Harding FPA.
- **Flash governor [GEN].** A local client subsystem counts the clipping impact flashes of the last 1 s. Once 3 have played, further impacts skip the clip-to-white frames and play at their in-flight values (§7.1 state limits) until the window clears. The frame-1 area flash still shows the exact radius, so readability holds. Cost ≈ 0 (a small ring buffer of timestamps). If §10 #15 still fails, also lower the frame-1 flash opacity under the governor (§12 Q40).
- **Edge cues are sustained ramps.** The out-of-zone overlay and the low-HP state (UI Guidelines §4.3) never pulse per damage tick, and the victim hit flash (§8) fires at most 2 times per second from damage-over-time sources.
- **Settings.**
  - "Screen flash effects" intensity, 0–100 %.
  - The existing "ally VFX intensity" (§7.2).
- **Before launch:** book a Harding test.

**UMG implementation rules [FACT, [UMG optimisation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/optimization-guidelines-for-umg-in-unreal-engine); local source]:**
- **Animation cost, cheapest first:** material-only ("no CPU cost"), then Blueprint tweens (low), then Sequencer (high CPU; render-transform tracks invalidate layout every frame). Use **material animations or tweens** for every per-hit or per-event pop.
- **No property bindings, no `OnTick` or `OnPaint`.** Bindings are polled every frame. Drive everything from events (GAS attribute and tag delegates).
- **Caching.** Wrap the static HUD in Invalidation Boxes, and test `Slate.EnableGlobalInvalidation=1` (default false, local `SlateCoreClasses.cpp:50`). Keep Canvas Panels and Overlays to a minimum, since they raise layer IDs and draw calls. Rich Text is "very expensive".
- **World-space health bars (6 max).** Either screen-space WidgetComponents with a fixed draw size, or one HUD-layer widget that projects positions. Never change `bDrawAtDesiredSize` per frame (local `WidgetComponent.h`).
- **No Niagara in the in-match HUD.**
  - **[FACT]** 5.8.3 ships an Epic "Niagara UI Renderer" plugin. It is `IsExperimentalVersion: true` and CPU-only, and `SNiagaraUIWidget::Tick` ticks one Niagara component per widget (local `Engine/Plugins/FX/NiagaraUIRenderer/`).
  - Allowed only in menus, hero select and victory screens, with at most 1–2 alive at once.
- **Damage-number alternative.** One Niagara Data Channel listener, `NDC_CombatFeedback`, rendering digits from an atlas. It can also carry the tertiary hit sparks. NDCs "optimize burst Niagara Systems by combining them into a large shared simulation" ([Epic NDC](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/niagara-data-channels-overview)). Choose by measurement (§12 Q27); the number style itself is in UI Guidelines §4.7. Primary layers and telegraphs stay as their own pooled cues, which are never culled.

**Perf summary.** Event-driven UMG plus material animations keeps the UI inside the 0.3 ms GPU budget (§3.0). It also removes the usual hidden game-thread cost of polling bindings on 6 bars and 4 ability slots.

## 13. How this guide evolves (taste log)

Most numbers in this bible are research-based starting points. The team's eye is the final judge, within the budget and the readability tests. Whenever someone reacts to a visual ("too saturated", "I love this fire", "the outline looks cheap"), the bible changes. The steps:

1. **Record it** in the taste log below, in the reviewer's own words: what was seen, the verdict, and why.
2. **Find the rules behind it.** Which sections and numbers produced the visual? Example: "too saturated" → §4.1 albedo saturation band, §7.1 core state limits.
3. **Check it against the higher precedence levels** (performance budget §3.0, readability tests §10).
   - **No conflict:** edit the rule in place, mark it **[TASTE]**, and point to the log entry (`[TASTE #3]`).
   - **Conflict:** don't apply it silently. Explain the conflict and propose the closest version that fits. Example: "less glow" is always fine; "more glow on every spell" is capped by the VisualWeight tiers and the overdraw budget.
4. **Tune the knob, not the asset.** Apply the change through parameters wherever possible: Material Instances, Curve Atlas rows, Toon Profiles, `DA_TeamColours`, Niagara user parameters. P5 exists to keep feedback cheap.
5. **Generalise patterns.** When two or more entries point the same way (e.g. "softer" twice), promote them to a general rule in the relevant section and note it in the log.
6. **Bump the version.** Add a line to the change log and increase the minor version (v0.2, v0.3, …).

Rules for the log:
- A [TASTE] entry beats research defaults. Only a later entry, or an explicit decision by the team, can reverse it.
- Entries are never deleted; a reversal is a new entry that points to the old one.
- "I don't like it" with no reason is still valid. Record it, and try **one** alternative at a time so the cause is clear.
- If the two team members disagree, log both views and mark the entry **open** until they decide.
